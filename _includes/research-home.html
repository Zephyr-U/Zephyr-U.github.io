How can a tiny, energy-constrained chip maintain an accurate measurement scale? I work on voltage references, self-calibration and temperature sensing, from physical error models to circuit implementation and silicon measurements.

How can sensing, power and communication work together? My current work develops biomedical ASICs and interfaces for bioimpedance and ultrasound, alongside collaborations in wearable physiological sensing.

Connecting the two: power-aware system design, AI-assisted circuit optimization, and emerging methods for assessing measurement reliability.

IEEE JSSC · 2024 · First author

Self-calibration for a stable measurement scale

A reference circuit acts as a measurement ruler. This work models related process and temperature errors, then couples on-chip adjustments to calibrate the reference at a single temperature.

±0.12% reported 1σ inaccuracy across −40 to 120 °C, measured over ten chips; sub-µW operation after calibration.

IEEE TCAS-I · 2026 · Corresponding author

Temperature sensing where area and energy are scarce

Two student-led studies address different constraints: compact thermal monitoring for AI processors, and temperature awareness during the cold start of energy-harvesting nodes.

I provided research guidance and am a corresponding author. Y. Zhang and H. Yu are the respective first authors.

Connecting sensing, power and two-way communication

I designed the bioimpedance and conversion circuits, power-management and monitoring circuits, and uplink/downlink circuits of a biomedical ASIC, covering architecture, implementation, layout and tapeout.
