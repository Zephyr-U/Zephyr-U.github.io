Research

Reliable measurements.

My research connects precision analog circuit design with low-power sensing systems. The starting point is a physical constraint; the goal is a measurement whose accuracy, energy cost and limitations can be demonstrated.

01 · Precision analog & sensor interfaces

A stable reference for a changing world

The question

How can a reference remain accurate across manufacturing variation and temperature without a large power budget or costly multi-temperature calibration?

The approach

I modelled how process variation affects reference voltage and temperature behaviour together, derived a coupled adjustment strategy, and implemented a digitally assisted calibration loop at one temperature.

Ten measured chips · −40 to 120 °C · reported 1σ inaccuracy reduced from ±0.53% to ±0.12% after calibration.

IEEE JSSC paper ↗

Related work explores switched-capacitor references, low-voltage operation, curvature compensation and AI-assisted multi-objective optimization. Together, these studies address the measurement scale that low-power sensors depend on.

02 · Biomedical sensing & integrated systems

A sensor needs more than a front end

A small biomedical device must establish its supply, acquire a useful signal, receive settings and return data. My Glasgow work brings these functions together in an integrated circuit.

Ongoing research

My contribution

I designed the Bio-Z and conversion circuits, power-management and monitoring circuits, and uplink/downlink circuits, including circuit architecture, implementation, layout and tapeout.

Current evidence

Power, receiver and transmitter blocks have hardware measurements. Bio-Z hardware characterization remains to be completed; full sensing-chain and application validation are subsequent milestones.

Devices · Interfaces · Collaboration

Ultrasound and physiological sensing

Ultrasound interfaces and imaging

My work includes research guidance on an ultrasound analog front end with bandwidth-enhanced current conveyance and continuous time-gain compensation; that manuscript is in resubmission.

Published collaborations include passive ultrasonic power/data transfer in IEEE TIE and piezoelectric transducers in ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces. I am a collaborating co-author on these studies.

I am also Co-PI of a UM-funded project on ASIC-enhanced photoacoustic blood-vessel imaging.

Wearable physiological measurements

I am a collaborating co-author of a wearable PPG system study in IEEE TBioCAS. This collaboration connects physiological acquisition, power management and wireless telemetry.

An emerging research direction examines how sensing conditions affect whether a physiological estimate is usable, linking hardware constraints with data-quality assessment.
